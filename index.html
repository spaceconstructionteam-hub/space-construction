WHAT NASA DID

At Bennu, OSIRIS-REx executed the sampling sequence autonomously. A signal from Earth took about 18.5 minutes to reach the spacecraft. Source: NASA

This is what the real mission did, step by step. These numbers come from NASA:

  • Sept 8, 2016 OSIRIS-REx launched on an Atlas V 411 rocket.
  • Oct 20, 2020 The sampler touched asteroid Bennu at the Nightingale site.
  • 1 s after contact It released a burst of nitrogen gas that stirred up loose rock and dust into the sampler head (TAGSAM).
  • First 3 s NASA’s initial data indicate that sampling took about five of the six seconds of contact, with most of the material collected in the first three seconds.
  • 6 s after contact It fired its thrusters to back away, while still sinking into Bennu.
  • 30 s after contact It shut off its thrusters and drifted away with the sample.
  • 70.3 g The first weight NASA measured (October 2023), already above the 60 g requirement.
  • 121.6 g The final sample returned to Earth. The mission needed at least 60 g, so it collected more than double. It is the largest asteroid sample ever collected in space.
  • About US$ 800 million The mission cost, not counting the launch vehicle.

NASA describes a science mission as four segments. You played all of them:

  • Launch your launch
  • Space the trip and the touch at Bennu
  • Ground Mission Control messages
  • User the scientists and the lab that open the capsule

BRAZIOSIRIS-REx is a fictional mission inspired by OSIRIS-REx. Credits, prices and fuel points are invented rules for this simulation. The game also has to pick a limit for staying on the surface (6.5 s), and it decides at which second you reach each real weight (3 s, 4.5 s and 6 s). Those are game rules. The weights themselves (60 g, 70.3 g, 121.6 g) are NASA numbers.
